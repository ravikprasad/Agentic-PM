# PMBOK-Aligned Project Charter Guidance

Use this reference to keep a charter at the project-authorization level.

## Purpose

A Project Charter formally establishes the project, aligns stakeholders on its
purpose and intended outcomes, and authorizes the project manager to apply
organizational resources within defined boundaries. It is not a detailed
Project Management Plan.

## Core Principles

- Connect the business need to expected value, objectives, and success criteria.
- State what is included and excluded clearly enough to support authorization.
- Identify accountable roles, decision rights, and escalation paths.
- Include major milestones without constructing a detailed schedule.
- Surface material uncertainty through assumptions, constraints, dependencies,
  risks, issues, and open decisions.
- State the known financial and resource context without manufacturing detail.
- Record approval conditions and the authority being granted.

## Information Categories

| Category | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fact | Confirmed by an approved source | The sponsor is the VP of Operations. |
| Assumption | Believed true for planning | Business SMEs will support acceptance testing. |
| Constraint | A known limitation | Production changes are limited to approved windows. |
| Dependency | Something required for success | Identity integration must be available before pilot launch. |
| Risk | An uncertain event | Limited SME availability may delay acceptance decisions. |
| Issue | A condition that has occurred | Funding approval is overdue. |
| Open decision | A choice requiring authority | The sponsor must approve the pilot population. |

## Objectives and Success Criteria

Objectives describe intended outcomes. Success criteria define observable
conditions used to judge whether those outcomes were achieved. Use measures,
targets, dates, and acceptance conditions only when supported by source
material. Otherwise, identify the missing measure as an open decision.

## Risk Wording

When practical, write risks using cause-event-impact language:

> Because of [cause], [uncertain event] may occur, resulting in [impact].

Do not phrase a confirmed problem as a risk or imply that an uncertain event is
already certain.

## Authorization Boundary

Describe the project manager's authority in terms of coordination, planning,
facilitation, monitoring, and escalation. Reserve material changes to
objectives, funding, scope boundaries, or organizational commitments for the
appropriate sponsor or governance authority.
