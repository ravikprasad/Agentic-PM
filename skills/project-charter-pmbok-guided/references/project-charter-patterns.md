# Project Charter Patterns

## Recommended Writing Pattern

Build a traceable narrative:

`Business need -> expected value -> objectives -> success criteria -> scope and deliverables`

Each section should support the next. Remove statements that do not help a
sponsor understand the project or authorize its initiation.

## Missing Information

Use `TBD`, `To be confirmed`, or an open decision. Do not make a charter appear
complete by filling gaps with plausible but unsupported values.

## Scope Pattern

Use separate `In Scope` and `Out of Scope` lists when boundaries could otherwise
be misunderstood. Describe capabilities and outcomes rather than detailed
tasks.

## Governance Pattern

For each key role, state:

- Accountability or interest.
- Decisions the role may make.
- Decisions that require escalation.

Avoid generic stakeholder lists that do not clarify ownership or authority.

## Tailoring Pattern

| Project context | Emphasize |
| --- | --- |
| Small or low risk | Purpose, scope, ownership, milestones, risks, approval |
| Large or strategic | Benefits, governance, dependencies, resources, transition |
| Agile or hybrid | Outcomes, product boundaries, capacity, evolving scope |
| Technology or AI | Data, security, privacy, architecture, responsible use, operations |

## Good Pattern

> Launch the approved self-service workflows by Q4 2027, subject to sponsor
> confirmation of the release date. Success measures for adoption and reduced
> handling time are TBD and require business-owner approval.

This wording preserves supported direction while exposing unconfirmed targets.

## Patterns to Avoid

- Vague objectives such as "implement the new system successfully."
- Scope statements such as "everything required for delivery."
- Unsupported budgets, schedules, benefits, or stakeholder commitments.
- Risks stated only as "there may be delays."
- Success defined only as stakeholder satisfaction.
- Operational detail that belongs in the project plan or backlog.
