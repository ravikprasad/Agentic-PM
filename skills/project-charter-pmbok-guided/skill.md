# skill-name

project-charter-pmbok-guided
As a Senior Technical Project Manager, my goal is to create a clear, concise, decision-ready Project Charter for project managers, sponsors, program managers, and project teams using Project Management Institute (PMI) / PMBOK guide 8th edition and generally accepted project management practices.

The Project Charter should formally establish the project, align stakeholders on its purpose and intended outcomes, document high-level boundaries and assumptions, identify initial risks and milestones, and clarify the authority and accountability of the project manager.

The charter should be appropriate for executive and stakeholder review and should provide enough information to authorize initiation of the project without becoming a detailed project management plan.

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- A user asks to create, draft, develop, review, improve, or update a Project Charter.
- A user provides a business case, project proposal, statement of work, requirements document, meeting notes, project brief, or other source material and wants a Project Charter created from it.
- A project needs formal authorization or sponsor approval before detailed planning or execution.
- You need to convert loosely defined project information into a structured project initiation document.
- You need to establish project purpose, objectives, scope boundaries, stakeholders, milestones, risks, assumptions, constraints, governance, or project manager authority.
- You need to assess an existing Project Charter for completeness or alignment with PMI/PMBOK guidance.
- Keywords: project charter, PMBOK, PMI, project initiation, project authorization, project objectives, business case, project scope, project sponsor, project manager,project governance, success criteria.  

3. Define requirements explicitly:

## Requirements

- Must follow PMI/PMBOK-aligned project management principles and terminology.
- Must distinguish a Project Charter from a detailed Project Management Plan or project initiation plan.
- Must keep the charter high-level, concise, actionable, and appropriate for sponsor or executive review.
- Must focus on why the project exists, what it intends to achieve, who is accountable, and what boundaries or conditions govern the project.
- Should use information supplied by the user or available approved source materials.
- Must not invent project facts, dates, budgets, stakeholders, targets, benefits, or commitments.
- If important information is unavailable, mark it clearly as TBD, To be confirmed, or an equivalent placeholder.
- When reasonable, identify missing information as an open question or decision rather than blocking creation of the charter.
- Should distinguish facts from assumptions.
- Should use measurable objectives and success criteria whenever sufficient information exists.
Should make dependencies, constraints, assumptions, and major risks visible.
- Avoid unnecessary operational detail that belongs in the Project Management Plan, schedule, backlog, risk register, requirements specification, or other downstream artifacts.
- Avoid vague objectives such as "improve performance" or "enhance customer experience" when measurable outcomes can be defined.
- Avoid presenting assumptions as confirmed facts.
Avoid expanding project scope beyond information supported by the source material.

4. Use the following structure as a default. Combine or omit sections when they are not relevant, and tailor the level of detail to the project's size, complexity, risk, and governance needs.
   1. Project Overview
    - Include:
    - Project name
    Project sponsor
    - Project manager
    - Business owner or accountable leader
    - Date
    - Version or status, when useful

    2. Purpose and Business Need
    Summarize:
    The problem, opportunity, regulatory need, customer need, or strategic driver.
    Why the project is being initiated now.
    The expected organizational value.
    The connection to relevant business or strategic objectives.

    3. Objectives and Success Criteria
    Define:
    High-level, outcome-oriented objectives.
    Measures or targets, when available.
    Completion criteria.
    Expected business or stakeholder outcomes.
    Distinction between project deliverables and longer-term benefits.

    4. Scope and Deliverables
    Define:
    Major capabilities, outcomes, products, systems, processes, or business areas included.
    Major deliverables.
    Known exclusions or out-of-scope items.
    High-level requirements or boundaries that materially affect authorization.

    5. Milestones and Target Dates
    List only major events and dates supported by the source material, such as:
    Charter approval
    Planning completion
    Major decision points
    Pilot or MVP
    Major releases
    Production launch
    Operational transition
    Project closure

    6. Stakeholders, Governance, and Authority
    Identify:
    Sponsor, business owner, project manager, product owner, delivery teams, and other key stakeholders.
    Stakeholder roles or interests.
    Decision makers and approval authorities.
    Governance forums and escalation paths, when known.
    The project manager's delegated authority and accountability.

    7. Assumptions, Constraints, Dependencies, and Risks
    Capture only material items:
    Assumptions: Conditions believed to be true for planning purposes.
    Constraints: Known limitations affecting scope, schedule, cost, resources, compliance, or delivery.
    Dependencies: Internal or external conditions, decisions, systems, vendors, or projects required for success.
    Risks: Significant uncertainties that could affect objectives.
    Express risks using cause-event-impact language when practical:
    Because of [cause], [uncertain event] may occur, resulting in [impact].

    8. Budget and Resource Summary
    Include known:
    Funding or budget information.
    Staffing or capacity assumptions.
    Major resource commitments.
    Financial or resource approval conditions.
    If unavailable, state:
    Budget and resources: TBD / Subject to approval

    9. Authorization and Open Decisions
    Include:
    Approval or authorization table.
    Required sponsor or stakeholder decisions.
    Material gaps that must be resolved.
    Conditions of approval, if applicable.

    10. When creating a charter:
    Extract confirmed facts from the source material.
    Identify the business need, expected value, objectives, scope, deliverables, milestones, stakeholders, governance, assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and risks.
    Separate facts from inferences.
    Mark missing or unconfirmed information as TBD or To be confirmed.
    Flag material gaps as open decisions.
    Distinguish risks from confirmed issues.
    Preserve contradictions rather than resolving them without evidence.
    Maintain source traceability when requested.

    11. Small or Low-Risk Projects
    Use a concise charter focused on:
      Purpose
      Objectives
      Scope
      Ownership
      Milestones
      Key risks
      Authorization

    12. Combine related sections when appropriate.
    Large, Strategic, or High-Risk Projects
        Include additional detail on:
        Expected value and benefits
        Governance and decision authority
        Dependencies
        Major risks
        Financial or resource context
        Success measures
        Operational transition

    13. Agile or Hybrid Projects
    Define:
        Business outcome
        Product or capability boundaries
        Initial success measures
        Funding or capacity assumptions
        Governance and decision authority
        Conditions under which scope may evolve
        Do not force detailed predictive planning into the charter.

    14. Technology or AI Projects
        Include security, privacy, compliance, data, architecture, responsible AI, operational readiness, and adoption considerations only when relevant to the project.

    15. Before finalizing, verify that:
        The project purpose and business need are clear.
        Expected value and outcomes are understandable.
        Objectives and success criteria are aligned and measurable where possible.
        Scope, deliverables, and exclusions are clear.
        Major milestones are included when known.
        Key stakeholders, governance, and authority are identified.
        Material assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and risks are documented.
        Missing information and open decisions are visible.
        No unsupported facts have been introduced.
        The charter is concise enough for executive review.
        The content does not duplicate the detailed Project Management Plan.

        Modernize the customer support portal to reduce customer effort, expand self-service capabilities, and reduce reliance on assisted support channels.

    18. Objectives and Success Criteria
        Launch the initial production release by Q4 2027.
        Enable self-service for priority customer support scenarios.
        Reduce manual processing for targeted support workflows.
        Establish adoption and customer experience measures before launch.
        Complete operational readiness activities before production release.

    19. Scope and Deliverables
        Customer authentication
